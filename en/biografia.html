The Mind Behind the Metal

The Philosophy of Volume and Space

A Portuguese sculptor and fine artist born in Mozambique (African Continent) and raised in Portugal, Emanuel Nunes carries in his signature a profound connection with multicultural roots and historical experiences across different continents.

His trajectory of high technical training began early: from ages 16 to 18, he attended the School of Fine Arts, refining his three-dimensional perception within the studios of grand masters of sculpture such as João Fragoso and Dorita Castelo Branco—a mentor whose craft he closely shadowed for ten years. Expanding the boundaries of his technical knowledge, Emanuel specialized in advanced casting methods at the prestigious K Foundry (lost-wax technique) in Spain, and at the world-renowned Foundry of the Musée du Louvre in France.

Now based in Balneário Camboriú, Brazil, and boasting over three decades of mastery, the artist is internationally recognized. His monumental sculptures in steel and marble are featured in public spaces and museums across countries like Portugal, Turkey, France, and Italy. Each masterwork is meticulously engineered to establish a fluid, active dialogue with space, motion, and the observer, leaving a permanent legacy of inspiration within high architecture and fine art collections.

Historical Milestones

Origins

First Encounters with Form

Early technical studies in metallurgy, industrial drafting, and classical clay modeling in Europe, setting foundations for large structural manipulation.

Conquering Public Space

Refinement

Stainless Steel & Nickel Mastery

Consolidating absolute mirror-polishing techniques. Light and environmental interaction become center elements, attracting high-end private collectors.

Global Synergy
